# Voice Template

**This is an add-on, not the main file.** The main file is
`custom-instructions-template.md` — it's the one that always goes in your
Project's Custom Instructions box. This voice guide defines how anything
drafted on your behalf should sound, and it feeds into that main file in
one of two ways:

1. **Attach as a separate file (recommended for length):** fill this in,
   save it, upload it to your Project as a knowledge file named
   `voice.md`. Leave the "My Voice" line in your main custom instructions
   pointing to it.
2. **Merge directly:** copy everything below into the "My Voice" section
   of `custom-instructions-template.md` instead of attaching a file.

Either works — pick whichever fits how you like to work.

## Step 1: Pick where you land on each of these

Circle or bold one side. There's no wrong answer — this is about matching
how you actually write, not how you think you "should" write.

- **Direct** ↔ Warm
- **Short and blunt** ↔ Detailed and explaining
- **Formal** ↔ Casual
- **Gets to the point immediately** ↔ Opens with context first
- **Uses bullet points often** ↔ Prefers full sentences/paragraphs

## Step 2: Write 3 rules in your own words

Look at what you circled above and turn it into instructions. Examples to
adapt (don't just copy these — write your own):

- "Be concise. Short sentences. No filler or corporate buzzwords."
- "Sound like a person, not a press release."
- "Always end with a clear next step and who owns it."

Your three rules:
1. [YOUR RULE 1]
2. [YOUR RULE 2]
3. [YOUR RULE 3]

## Step 3: Paste one real example

Paste a short email, Slack message, or note you've actually written that
sounds like you. This single example often teaches tone better than any
list of adjectives.

```
[PASTE YOUR REAL EXAMPLE HERE]
```

## Step 4 (optional): Different modes for different audiences

If you write differently to your boss than to your team than to an
external partner or client, say so explicitly. Example structure:

- **To leadership/executives:** shorter, focused on decisions and risk
- **To my team:** slightly more context, focused on execution
- **To external partners/clients:** more polished, still direct but more
  diplomatic

Your modes (delete if you only need one voice):
- **[AUDIENCE 1]:** [HOW YOU ADAPT]
- **[AUDIENCE 2]:** [HOW YOU ADAPT]

---

**Facilitator note (delete before using):** in the session, Step 3 is the
highest-leverage 2 minutes in the whole exercise. A single real writing
sample does more than any amount of describing tone in the abstract.
